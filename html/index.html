        How to write a game
        
Describe a game by its rules: who may make which move when, what it changes, what it pays, and who moves
            next. The page plays out every possible way the game can go, and CheckMate checks the tree that results.

        
players: Alice, Bob  # a comment after a single name describes it
constants: price, cost
initial: price > cost

start: Alice
state: offered = no

honest move offer by Alice      # a comment here describes the move
  set offered = yes
  next Bob

honest move accept by Bob
  if offered
  pay Alice: price - cost, Bob: 0
  end

move reject by Bob
  pay Alice: -cost
  end

        
    
            
  • Declare the players:, and the symbols in payoffs as constants: (or infinitesimals:, for
                amounts smaller than any constant); initial: lines constrain them.
    • 
            
  • start: says who moves first, and state: lists what the game keeps track of, with
                its starting values: yes/no, numbers, or names.
    • 
            
  • A move belongs to one or more players (by Alice, Bob; then me is the one who
                moves, and with two players other is the other one). Below it: if conditions on
                the state, set to change it, pay to add to payoffs, and then
                next PLAYER or end.
    • 
            
  • Mark the moves of the intended play with honest. Where several honest moves are possible,
                each of the resulting plays is checked in turn.
    •
